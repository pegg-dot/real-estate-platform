## What's genuinely NEW vs the prior Pace episode (build/enhance targets)

1. **Installment-sale tax precision (enhance 004 cap-gains modeler).** Seller on seller-finance
   pays tax only on principal **actually received each year**, not the whole price at closing; plus
   **depreciation recapture** (can push taxable amount toward the full price but "gets to zero, not
   below"). The 1031 alternative is rejected by tired sellers ("if I 1031 I'm not retired anymore").
   → Make the seller-win modeler model installment recognition + recapture, not a single cap-gains hit.

2. **Long-amortization seller finance (enhance 004 + 007A).** Mario deal: $3M, **0 down, 4% fixed,
   50-year amortization** → $11k/mo → ~$6.6M over 600 months. Long am = low payment = cash flow now;
   the structure space should include long/zero-am terms.

3. **Toxic-debt check for multifamily subject-to (enhance 004).** ⭐ Single-family sub2 is safe
   (30-yr fixed 2–4%); **multifamily often carries short-term/adjustable "toxic" debt — a ticking
   time bomb.** Rule: when recommending sub2 on multifamily, **gate on debt type** (fixed + long
   term OK; short-term/adjustable → exclude/flag). Sweet spot = 50–250 units, built '70s–2000s,
   owned 20–30 yrs (debt paid down).

4. **The wrap / "become the bank" disposition (NEW module — see capability map "gator/lending").**
   Take a property subject-to (keep the $50k 1st-position loan), then **create a 2nd note (e.g.
   $150k) and seller-finance it to a "penalty-box buyer"** (an entrepreneur who can't get a bank
   loan). Pace becomes the bank in 2nd position. A disposition + lending play, not just acquisition.

5. **State landlord-law filter for market selection (NEW market-level gate).** Choose markets by
   **landlord-friendliness FIRST** (avoid OR/WA/CA/NY/NJ/MD; CO turning unfriendly), then migration
   / population growth / birth rate. → A market gate feeding market scoring + Miami/expansion logic.

6. **Macro distress-timing radar (enhance 012-distress).** 2023–26 thesis: maturing/expiring debt +
   adjustable-rate resets + rising insurance + blowing cap rates → a **multifamily distress wave**;
   sellers trade to operators *before* foreclosure because cash flow is valuable. → A macro signal
   layer ("debt maturing / rate reset / insurance spike") that flags a coming-distress window.

7. **Payment-structured-around-the-seller's-life-need (NEW coach exemplar for 015).** Don't compute
   the payment from a spreadsheet — set it to the seller's **actual monthly need**: Susan's RV-spot
   cost ($375/mo → "I'll pay you $375/mo"); Mario's son's annuity. The number serves the bunny.

8. **Deed vs mortgage vs title (NEW teaching primitive → 016 + the coach + the UI explainer).**
   Ownership = the **deed** (the receipt), NOT the debt. The bank holds a *mortgage* (a promise to
   pay), not ownership. Analogies: groceries bought on a credit card (you own the groceries; the
   card is a promise to pay); a co-signed car (you own it, mom co-signed). This is the conceptual
   core that makes subject-to make sense to a skeptic.

9. **Lease-option / master-lease vs subject-to (enhance 007/004 rationale).** Pace rejects master
   leases/lease options for himself because **without fee title you can't take depreciation** (no
   deed = no depreciation/cost-seg). Lease-option's option price can be set to the mortgage balance
   at execution — same economics, but no tax benefits. → Encode *why* sub2/seller-finance beats
   lease-option for a tax-motivated buyer.

10. **Due-on-sale real-world calibration (data point for the 004 guardrail — keep the guardrail).**
    One Texas title company: **~10,000 sub2 transactions in 35 years, ~10 called due, 0 ever had to
    be paid off.** Extremely rare in practice; bank has no involvement (deed and mortgage are
    separate instruments), enforcement = judicial foreclosure (slow, costly). *Keep the honest
    guardrail* (the risk is real and rate-environment-elevated) but record this as a cited frequency
    datapoint, attributed.

## Exemplars (→ knowledge_exemplar, for the 015 coach)
- **The F-150 terms-explainer** (Pace's standard, never-changed analogy to explain "terms" to any
  seller: KBB said $5k → sold on payments for $12.5k → "your house is worth 40, you want 100; let me
  give you terms").
- **The "common enemy" expired-listing opener:** *"We noticed your house went off market — what was
  your agent not able to get you that you were looking for?"* (creates a shared enemy, opens the seller up).
- **The foreclosure/subject-to script:** *"You're behind / going through a divorce — we'll catch up
  your payments, put a couple grand in your hand, take over the payments and deed it to us. It's
  called subject-to. You down?"*
- **Emotional/legacy structuring (Mario):** turn a 5-yr-balloon demand into a 50-yr annuity for the
  seller's son — *"when your son is 62 he gets a check from Pace every month; turn this property into
  a memory you set up."*
- **Payment-around-need (Susan):** ask what their life costs ("$375/mo for the RV spot") and set the
  payment to that.
- **The "why do I need the bank's permission?" reframe** (Pace flips Grant's "why would the bank let
  you?" into the better question).

## TWO persona profiles (→ expert_profile)

### Pace Morby — the Operator / Closer
- **Values:** control of debt > cash; cash flow ("forever money"); solving the seller's emotional
  "bunny"; speed; never bring cash to acquire ("cash deals are poisonous — bring cash only for capex").
- **Heuristics:** find the tired/old seller, read micro-signals (the 12-yr-old who hates the
  property), structure terms around the bunny, prefer subject-to/seller-finance/wrap, keep fee title
  for depreciation, avoid toxic multifamily debt.
- **Voice:** storytelling, empathetic, mechanic-of-deals; teaches with analogies (F-150, groceries).

### Grant Cardone — the Scaler / Skeptic / Questioner
- **Values:** scale ("how do we do 1,000 a month?"), 10x, capital deployment, legality/defensibility,
  macro timing, owning real estate as the asset class.
- **Heuristics:** challenge every mechanism from first principles; verify legality; ask who *owns*
  it; ask how it scales; find the pain/catch; compare alternatives (Roku vs Warner; lease vs sub2).
- **Voice:** blunt, probing, big-picture; turns claims into "is that legal / who owns it / why would
  they agree / how do we scale."

## Grant's Question Bank (→ the Deal Interrogation engine, spec 020)
The great diligence questions, generalized so the platform can ask them of any deal:
- Is this legal? (legality + where it's documented — IRS pages, Fannie guidelines, state CE forms)
- Who actually *owns* this — does the bank own it? (ownership vs debt)
- Why would the counterparty (bank / seller / agent) agree to this? What's in it for them?
- Have you ever had the downside happen (due-on-sale called)? How often? What happened?
- Why wouldn't you do it the other way (lease option / master lease / call the agent direct / get a loan)?
- How many of these can you do — can it scale? What's the bottleneck?
- Where does the money go / where's the pain / why would they walk away?
- How do you do due diligence on a new market? (landlord law → migration → growth)
- What's the catch / the downside / the toxic version of this?
- How do we get from X to 10X (e.g., $300k/mo → $3M/mo cash flow)?
