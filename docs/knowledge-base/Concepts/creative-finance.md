# Concept: Creative Finance (the core thesis)

The argument that you can acquire real estate without traditional bank financing, and
that on motivated-seller situations this beats cash because it's **positive-sum**.

---

## Traditional/cash is zero-sum
Cash investors buy ~50¢ on the dollar. Your discount comes directly out of the seller's
pocket. Most sellers won't accept that, so cash can only solve a minority of situations.

## The equity reality (why so many sellers are stuck)  ⭐
**Equity ≠ Zillow value − loan balance.** True equity = what's left *after* the cost to
sell, which averages **~10%** (both agents' commissions, title, inspection credits,
lender concessions).

→ A seller who "owes $381k on a $400k house" has **negative** equity. Sold traditionally
they'd net ~$360k and have to **write a ~$21k check** to close.

Pace's figures (verify): ~6M US owners hold 2–4% mortgages with little/no equity;
~20%+ of current sellers write a check to sell; ~900/mo in Maricopa County.

## NEED vs. GREED — pick the structure  ⭐
- **NEED → Subject-To (sub2):** seller has no equity / is distressed. You take over
  their existing low-rate mortgage, no money down. From their side you effectively
  *gave* them the ~$21k check they'd otherwise write.
- **GREED → Seller Finance:** seller has equity and wants top dollar. They finance the
  purchase to you over time → higher price + monthly income + **capital-gains deferral**
  (huge for older owners of long-held appreciated assets) and you skip the bank.

## The ~26 strategies (umbrella terms only — expand as future sources cover them)
Subject-To, Seller Finance, Hybrids (sub2 + seller carry), Morby Method, Lease Options,
agreements for sale / contracts for deed / bond for deed, "innovation" & "dating"
agreements. Common thread: **creative finance solves more "bunnies"** than cash —
especially price, no-equity, and tax problems cash can't touch.

## Where the money comes from in these deals — "gators"
A **gator** is a private lender for the small, short-term gaps creative deals need:
**EMD** (earnest money deposit), down payments, hard/private money, refinance money.
Lending is "today money you can do forever."

---

## Worked structures (from sources)
- **161-unit, Tucson (seller finance, GREED):** $19.5M price; $5M down (pooled from
  investors); $14.5M carried by seller at 4% for 15 yrs. ~$165k/mo rents → ~$46k/mo to
  seller + opex → **~$80k/mo net.** No bank.
- **Sub2 logic (NEED):** take the 2–4% mortgage on a no-equity house; payments stay low
  because the rate is locked; seller escapes the check + the move.
*(Source: Pace Morby)*

---

## Risks & cautions (research before doing one — especially with trust money)
- **Due-on-sale clause:** Subject-To leaves the seller's mortgage in place; lenders can
  (rarely, but legally) call the loan due on transfer. Must be understood before using
  sub2.
- **State regulation:** wholesaling is licensed/banned in ~11 states; creative-finance
  disclosure rules vary. Verify per target market.
- **Insurance, title, and trust-ownership mechanics** for sub2/seller-finance need a
  real attorney — doubly so when a family trust is the buyer.

→ These are flagged as **must-research** items, not settled facts.
