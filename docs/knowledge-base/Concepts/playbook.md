# Concept: The Core Playbook (frameworks & decision models)

Cross-source distillation of *how to think* about real estate strategy. Append new
sources here; note disagreements rather than overwriting.

---

## The four umbrellas
Every real estate role is one of: **Wholesale · Develop · Buy & Hold · Service.**
First three = investor (you own/control the deal); Service = supports the deal.
*(Source: Pace Morby)*

## Exit strategy ≠ deal source
"Section 8," "Airbnb," "fix & flip" describe how you *make money on* a property, not
how you *found or financed* it. Single-strategy mentors teach the exit and skip the
sourcing — which is the hard part. Always separate: **Sourcing → Financing → Exit.**
*(Source: Pace Morby)*

## "You-investor," not "new-investor"  ⭐ key model
Don't ask "what should a beginner do?" Ask "what should *I*, given my situation, do?"
Decide along two axes:
1. **Time vs. Money** — no money → spend time (door-knock, bird dog); no time → spend
   money (paid inbound leads).
2. **Urgency / risk** — need cash now → quick-win plays (wholesale, bird dog, orbit);
   stable income + patience → buy-and-hold acquisition on the side.
*(Source: Pace Morby)*

## Today / Tomorrow / Forever money
Classify any strategy by *when it pays*:
- **Today** = wholesale, bird dog, flip, lending, agent (transactional/active).
- **Tomorrow** = development, BRRRR.
- **Forever** = buy-and-hold portfolio, multifamily, RV/MH parks (the real goal).
Lending = today-money you can do forever. Wholesale/agent = the "9-to-5 of real estate."
*(Source: Pace Morby)*

## Bunnies — sell to motivation, not to the house  ⭐ key model
Every deal traces to a seller's *emotional* reason to sell (foreclosure, probate,
divorce, death, relocation, retirement, capital-gains exposure, landlord burnout).
You don't negotiate — you find the bunnies and solve them. "I just want to sell" = no
motivation = no deal. Resistance on a call = you haven't earned the real story yet.
*(Source: Pace Morby)*

## NEED vs. GREED → which creative tool
- **NEED** (no equity / distress) → **Subject-To**.
- **GREED** (has equity, wants top price + tax deferral) → **Seller Finance**.
*(Source: Pace Morby — see `creative-finance.md`)*

### Why college towns are a coherent strategy (and what's unique about them)
- **Durable demand:** steady enrollment = reliable tenant pipeline; recession-resilient.
- **Co-living / by-the-room is the supercharger:** student rentals often pencil best
  rented per-bedroom, not per-house (see exit strategies). A 4-bed near campus can
  out-earn a same-priced SFR rented whole.
- **The critical risk to underwrite — occupancy/zoning ordinances:** many college towns
  legally cap the number of *unrelated* tenants per dwelling (e.g. "no more than 3
  unrelated"), which directly kills or makes a by-the-room deal. This must be checked
  per-city, per-zone, *before* buying. (Charlottesville and Tempe both have rules here.)
- **Other must-track variables:** distance/walkability to campus, enrollment trend &
  on-campus housing supply, parking, turnover/management intensity (students = higher
  churn, summer vacancy), parents as co-signers/guarantors (lowers credit risk).
- **Motivated sellers near campus:** long-tenured "tired landlords" who've run student
  houses for decades are prime DTS/seller-finance targets.
