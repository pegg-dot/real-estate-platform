# Concept: Lead Generation — where deals actually come from

Every deal starts with a **motivated seller**. The work is (1) pick a motivation type,
(2) get a list, (3) reach out, (4) qualify. Lists are not outreach — a probate list
won't call anyone for you.

---

## The three acquisition channels
- **DTS — Direct to Seller** — off-market; mail/call/text/door-knock the owner.
- **DTA — Direct to Agent** — call listing agents on stale on-market listings.
- **DTR — Direct to Referral** — probate/tax attorneys referring distressed families.

**On-market ≠ callable.** Zillow/Crexi/LoopNet listings are agent-controlled (listing
agreement = agent gets paid regardless). Don't call those sellers directly.

---

## Motivation / "list" types (seller pain points)
| List type | Why they're motivated |
|---|---|
| **Probate** | Inherited property, no estate plan → forced through probate (~900/mo in Maricopa County) |
| **Expired listings** | Tried to sell, failed |
| **Foreclosure** | Distress, time pressure |
| **Tired landlords** | Owned 1–3 rentals 15+ yrs, self-managed, burned out, capped at their capacity |
| **Driving for dollars** | Visibly distressed/ugly houses — but ugly ≠ motivated; weakest signal |

## The actual tools named (and rough costs)
> These are the specific vendors Pace named. Verify current pricing/relevance before
> subscribing — captured 2026-05-31 from a talk referencing older numbers.

- **Probate leads:** `alltheleads.com` (~$600/mo)
- **Foreclosure leads:** `dealsauce.io` (~$97/mo) + skip-trace (~12¢/number)
- **Expired listings:** `Vulcan 7` (~$99/mo)
- **Free alternative:** county recorder's office (foreclosures recorded daily) — free
  but time-expensive.
- **Mail vendor mentioned (negatively):** Yellow Letters HQ — Pace says don't use them;
  he later learned they just resold the same data (DealSauce/BatchLeads/AllTheLeads) at
  a markup.

*(Source for all of the above: Pace Morby)*

## Inbound vs. outbound
- **Inbound** (seller responds to your mailer/ad and calls you) = higher intent, higher
  quality. Skilled wholesalers migrate toward inbound-only over time.
- **Outbound** (you cold-call foreclosures, etc.) = cheaper to start, lower intent.
- Cost reality: Phoenix inbound was ~$400/call in 2012, ~$2,000/call now — but still
  worth it if the deal nets tens of thousands.

## Outreach methods (turning a list into a conversation)
Mailers/postcards, PPC (pay-per-click), PPL (pay-per-lead — someone else's PPC resold),
cold calling (often VAs), texting, and door-knocking. Door-knocking is highest-friction
but high-yield (Pace claims ~100 foreclosure doors reliably produces a deal).

---

## Practical takeaway for a buy-and-hold / trust-capital investor
- The highest-leverage channels for *forever-money* acquisitions are **DTA on stale
  listings** (motivated sellers + agents who just want it closed) and **tired-landlord
  / older-owner DTS** (e.g. RV parks, small multifamily owned 20–35 yrs) — these are
  where seller-finance structures land best (see `creative-finance.md`).
- You don't need the wholesaler's high-volume cold-call machine. You need a *narrow,
  repeatable* pipeline into a few motivated, high-equity older owners per quarter.
