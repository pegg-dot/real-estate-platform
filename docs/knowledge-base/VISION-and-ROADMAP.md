# Vision & Roadmap — the "AI Real Estate Tool"

Honest strategic take on the bigger idea Nate floated: an all-in-one, AI-native real
estate tool — a Google-Maps-style view of listings enriched with data from YouTube,
podcasts, and other sources, each property scored/rated, auto-updating, with lead
generation/automation layered on. The goal: *help Nate's actual process of buying and
building a portfolio with family-trust capital* — not to ship a startup for its own sake.

---

## The honest read

**The instinct is right; the framing is the trap.** "All-in-one Google Maps of every
listing with ratings + lead automation" is, in 2026, a *crowded* and *capital-heavy*
space. Companies with hundreds of millions in funding already do pieces of it:
- **Map + data over listings:** Zillow, Redfin, PropStream, Reonomy, Realeflow.
- **Motivated-seller lists + skip trace:** PropStream, BatchLeads, DealMachine,
  Privy, the niche tools from source 1 (AllTheLeads, DealSauce, Vulcan7).
- **Lead automation / CRM + dialers:** REISift, Launch Control, Lofty, Follow Up Boss.

Trying to out-build all of that is how you end up with a worse, more expensive copy.
And bolting GPT onto a map is exactly the "GPT wrapper" you said you *don't* want.

**Where the actual, defensible edge is — and it's the thing you already half-described:**
> *the knowledge layer.* Nobody has a good product that ingests the firehose of
> real-estate *education and reasoning* (podcasts, YouTube, deal breakdowns, gurus
> contradicting each other) and turns it into a **personal, cross-source knowledge
> graph that then advises you on a specific deal in front of you.** The listing-data
> tools are commodities. Your *synthesized judgment layer* on top of them is not.

That's why we started with the Knowledge Base folder. It's not a consolation prize —
it **is** the wedge. The map is the easy, fundable, commoditized part. The brain is the
hard, rare part, and you can start building it today with zero engineering.

---

## Reframed product: "The Operator's Brain," in three layers

1. **Knowledge layer (BUILD FIRST — already started).**
   Structured, linked notes from every podcast/video/book → a cross-source model of
   strategy, financing, and tactics, personalized to a buy-and-hold / trust-capital
   goal. This is `Knowledge Base/`. Compounds with every source you add. Defensible
   because it's *your* synthesized judgment, not public data.

2. **Deal layer (NEXT).**
   A simple, structured pipeline of properties *you're actually evaluating* — address,
   numbers, the seller's "bunnies," which financing structure fits (sub2 vs. seller
   finance vs. cash), and a score. Start as a spreadsheet or a few markdown files;
   only later does it need to be an app. The AI value: it reasons over a deal *using
   your knowledge layer* ("this is a tired-landlord RV park, owner is 72 — here's the
   seller-finance + cap-gains pitch, here's the under-write").

3. **Sourcing/automation layer (LATER, and mostly buy-not-build).**
   Lead lists, skip trace, outreach. This is where existing tools are good and cheap —
   you should *subscribe*, not rebuild. AI's role is triage and message-drafting, not
   reinventing PropStream.

The order matters: most people try to build layer 3 first (the shiny automation) and
have nothing intelligent to point it at. Layers 1→2 first means by the time you touch
automation, it's aimed by real judgment.

---

## Phased roadmap

**Phase 0 — Knowledge base (DONE / ongoing).**
Folder live. Add every source going forward. Zero cost, compounds immediately.

**Phase 1 — Personalize the model (next few sessions).**
Pin down the real strategy: target markets, asset types (RV/MH parks? small
multifamily? SFR rentals?), how the trust deploys capital, risk limits, timeline.
Turn `playbook.md`'s "Nate's profile" from a sketch into a real thesis. Research the
trust + creative-finance legal questions flagged in `creative-finance.md`.

**Phase 2 — Deal pipeline + underwriting (when you have live deals).**
A structured deal tracker (spreadsheet or artifact) + an underwriting workflow that
scores a property and recommends a financing structure, citing the knowledge base.
This is where AI stops being a chat and starts being an analyst.

**Phase 3 — Sourcing, mostly via existing tools.**
Pick ONE lead channel that fits the thesis (likely DTA on stale listings, or
older-owner DTS for parks/multifamily), subscribe to the right existing tool, and use
AI for triage + outreach drafting. Measure before scaling.

**Phase 4 — Only if there's a real, proven edge: productize.**
If the knowledge+deal layer is genuinely giving you an advantage, *then* consider
wrapping it for others. Build the tool because it already works for you — never before.

---

## Decisions that shape everything (open — for Nate)
- **Asset focus:** what are you actually trying to buy first? (RV/MH parks, small
  multifamily, SFR rentals, land...) This single answer reorders the whole roadmap.
- **Target geography:** one market to go deep, or opportunistic/national?
- **Trust mechanics:** how does the family trust want to deploy — all-cash, leverage,
  partner with others? (Heavily affects whether creative finance is even needed.)
- **Your role:** active operator, or passive capital allocator? (Determines how much
  sourcing/automation you ever need.)
- **Build appetite:** are you genuinely interested in building software, or do you just
  want the *outcome* (good deals)? If it's the outcome, lean "buy tools + AI brain,"
  not "build a platform."

These are the questions that turn this from a cool idea into a plan.
